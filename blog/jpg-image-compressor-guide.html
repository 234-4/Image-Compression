JPG Image Compressor: How to Compress JPG Files Online

Published November 07, 2026 · 5 min read

A JPG image compressor shrinks the file size of your photos so they upload faster, fit in emails and take up less space. The trick is doing it without making the picture look worse. This guide explains how JPG compression works, which quality setting to choose, and how to get a smaller file you are happy with.

How JPG compression works

JPG is a lossy format. When a JPG is saved, the software discards small details that the eye is unlikely to notice, and the lower the quality setting, the more it discards. That is why a JPG can be a fraction of the size of the same picture saved as a PNG. Push the setting too low, though, and you start to see blocky patches and fuzzy edges around text and sharp lines.

For most photos, 70–85% gives a noticeably smaller file with a difference that is hard to spot on a screen. Always check the preview before you download.

How to avoid losing quality

  • Resize first if the photo is huge. A 4,000-pixel photo shown at 1,200 pixels wastes data, and resizing removes it more cleanly than heavy compression does.
  • Compress once. Every time a JPG is re-saved, a little more detail is lost. Go back to the original rather than compressing a compressed copy.
  • Keep the original. Save the smaller version as a separate file.
  • Be careful with text. Screenshots and scanned documents suffer most from JPG compression, and PNG often suits them better. Our format guide explains the difference.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best JPG compression quality?

For most photos, 70–85% gives a much smaller file with a difference that is hard to notice on a screen. Use a higher setting for photos you want to keep sharp.

Can I compress a JPG without losing any quality?

JPG is lossy, so some detail is always removed. At moderate settings the loss is usually not visible, but it is never exactly zero.

How much can a JPG be compressed?

It depends on the photo. Large phone photos can often be reduced to a fraction of their size, while images that are already well optimized may not shrink much.

Is it safe to use an online JPG compressor?

It is safest with a tool that works in your browser, so your photo never leaves your device. Check where a tool sends your files before using it with private images.
